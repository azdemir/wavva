# Outreach Script — finding pilot companies

## Who to call

Independent residential cleaning companies in Upstate NY with 2–15 people:
Albany, Schenectady, Troy, Saratoga Springs, Syracuse, Rochester, Utica areas.
Find them via Google Maps ("house cleaning near …"), local Facebook groups,
and Thumbtack/Yelp listings. Solo cleaners with growth ambitions are ideal —
they feel scheduling pain most acutely.

## The call (3 minutes)

> Hi, I'm [name] — I'm building TidyPro, scheduling and invoicing software
> made specifically for cleaning companies around here. I'm looking for two
> or three local companies to pilot it free for a month and tell me what's
> broken.
>
> Quick question: how are you handling scheduling and quotes right now —
> is it mostly texts and spreadsheets?

Listen. Then:

> That's exactly what we're fixing — quotes, bookings, crew schedules, and
> invoices in one place, plus a link your customers can use to approve quotes
> and pay online. The pilot is free, I set everything up with you on a
> 30-minute call, and you keep your data either way. Would [day] work for a
> quick setup call?

## If they hesitate

- "I don't have time to learn software" → "That's the point of the pilot —
  I do the setup with you, 30 minutes, and your cleaner just opens a simple
  job list on their phone."
- "What does it cost after?" → "Pilot is free for [30/60] days. After that,
  paid plans start at $49/month — but you only pay if it's actually saving
  you time."
- "Is my customer data safe?" → "Each company gets a fully isolated
  workspace — your customers, schedules, and invoices are invisible to
  everyone else, enforced in the database."

## Follow-up text (same day)

> [Name], great chatting! Here's my number — reply with a good time for the
> 30-min setup call and I'll send the calendar invite. — [Your name], TidyPro

## Tracking

Keep a simple sheet: company, owner, phone, called date, outcome, follow-up
date. Call every lead twice before dropping them.
